This reflection has been something I have had on my mind for a long time now. Since 2024 actually, when I did a class course on e-publishing. This piece is a reflection on the relevance of books. I will attempt to emphasise the need for reading books in two contexts both as a technologist and as Nigerian.

Books, books, books. If you’re a Gen Z, you will understand that there has been a lot of talk about Gen Z not reading enough if at all. I, myself, am not the best of readers in terms of consistency and volume covered. I am slow in reading and abandon some books at times. However, I still find books very relevant. With the growth of social media, there has been some optimism that it will displace the position of books as the most important source of knowledge. I disagree with this context and will also layout my argument for books in this piece. I focus specifically on text driven social platforms like X and long form video platforms like Youtube. I’ll be pointing some issues with social media in terms of knowledge production in the next section.

Social media attempts to summarise complex ideas into short posts. Imagine *Things Fall Apart* by Chinua Achebe being shortened into 280 character/ 50 word limit of the Twitter free plan. There’s no way you can have the richness of thought contained in the book on social media. At best, you’ll have a snapshot of it. Social media is excellent for getting instantaneous reaction not systematic reflection. That is why you will observe that notable people usually write books about their experience. Books give a certain kind of permanence that social media posts don’t give. The regular user finds it easier to locate a book than locate a singular post among thousands. There’s no doubt that social media is a useful tool for understanding peoples perspectives of present occurrences but if you want to understand the past in a structured manner, you usually have to find the information within books.

Furthermore, I will argue from the aspect of creative control and democratic access. It has never been easier to publish a book. The same way anyone can sit down from the comfort of their room and post a video on Youtube is the same way he could publish a book online on Amazon KDP or Selar. Authors exercise large amounts of creative control in writing their stories. They are free to present their books in any structure possible contrary to popular assumptions.

While there is an argument that social media gives a large amount of creative control, long form content contained in books gives free expression to one's ideas. Even in our fast paced, short attention span world, depth is still a treasure and is one of the elements books still have. Although you can find 12 hour content on Youtube, the platform is not natively designed for that length of content. There are some people that can only watch a 30 minutes max video on Youtube but you have to do that if you are reading books. Although with the growth of Artificial Intelligence tools, there’s the tendency to just throw the book into ChatGPT or NotebookLM and get a summary. However, these tools take away the satisfaction of watching an interesting story unfold before one’s eyes. We still need books to enable free expression of ideas without recourse to maximising for recommendation by an algorithm.

With this said, I will be moving to the relevance of books and reading for tech bros

Being a tech bro especially in the age of advanced coding tools has never made reading books less attractive, each iteration of Claude, Gemini and GPT Codex push the code generation capability of AI models. However, books still help with learning new paradigms and tools. The biggest complaint I can see from a tech book is that they might get obsolete. That only happens when the focus of the book is on tools not the underlying process and system guiding the usage of the tool. An example to contrast it could be a book on UI design versus a book on Tailwind CSS. Once the focus is placed on the process, it becomes obvious that the depth provided by books is unmatched. The closest replica to a book can only be found on Youtube. That’s why the focus is on having practical implementation videos on Youtube as tools are constantly in flux. Books provide a structure that is often needed for learning new things systematically as opposed to Youtube. If you want to understand a concept and learn on your own, I recommend starting with a book as it provides a guide you can follow for structured learning.

Documentation (docs) is a close sibling to books for a developer. Documentation is the living description of the entire system. Ideally when using a tool or programming language for the first time, the first piece of advice is to "read the docs” to understand what a tool can and can’t do. Without a long form content of record, software teams collapse under the increasing scale of the solutions they create. Documentation is also useful for AI agents as it provides them the context they need to write functioning code.

After reflecting on the importance of books for technologists, I will now focus on its importance for Nigerians and storytelling across the continent. Books give African authors the ability to capture the stories of their people clearly. This extends to both fiction and non-fiction books. Think of the civil war conflict captured by Chimamanda Adichie’s *Half of a Yellow Sun.* There is a great benefit in telling one’s stories. It is even more crucial in the AI age. LLMs (think ChatGPT) are trained on mainly text data. Books make a huge portion of the data used to teach AI models to reason like humans. If we stop writing and reading books, there’s a huge possibility of losing a lot of information about ourselves. We have already seen that oral history is limited by the importance of transmission from one generation to the next. Writing solves this issue as it ensures that people can always discover the information in a permanent form. We need to write so our stories can shape the AI models of today and in future. If we do not write or read, we risk creating models with bias towards us while also bringing up new generations of Africans to believe false narratives about themselves and their people. This wave of authorship must be championed by brilliant African authors who try as much as possible to capture African stories authentically and encouraged more by an eager to read population.

To conclude, books sparked a revolution in Western Europe. If more Africans embrace books and book publishing, there is no doubt that they can spark a revolution of progress in the continent. Next time, you are a bored and about to doom scroll, consider reading a book instead, you will be better for it.
