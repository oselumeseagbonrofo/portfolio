Nigeria has been plagued by rising poverty, increased ethnic tensions and bad governance. Amidst these challenges, the internet has become a unifying factor among youths. 

Digital platforms have allowed movements such as the \#OccupyNigeria, \#EndSARS and \#Obidient movement to grow and mobilise in previously impossible ways. Facebook and X (formerly Twitter) served as rallying points for these youth movements in their struggle against oppressive governments. These platforms were used to mobilise support for those movements and provide live updates on the movement's activities. In particular, the truth of the Lekki toll gate massacre may be unknown if people like DJ Switch had not broadcast it live on their social media platforms. Till today, the government continues to deny its occurrence. 

The internet served as the nexus for all three movements. It allowed youths to express their views on the government and other institutions despite their lack of access to official grievance channels. Also, each movement represented a struggle by young people to change their society positively. The internet enabled these movements to attain widespread support very quickly. 

In conclusion, digital platforms have an immense impact on society. ‘The pen is mightier than the sword’ is a common saying. However, in today’s world, I argue that X is mightier than a nuclear weapon due to its ability to mass mobilise people for social change advocacy.
